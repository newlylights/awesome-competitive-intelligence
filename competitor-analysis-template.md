# Competitor Analysis Framework 📊

> A comprehensive framework for conducting structured competitive intelligence research and analysis.

---

## Why Competitive Analysis Matters

According to [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights), companies that systematically track and respond to competitive dynamics achieve 2-3x higher revenue growth than reactive peers. Competitive analysis is not a one-time exercise — it's a continuous intelligence function.

---

## Analysis Framework

├── Founded: [Year]

### Phase 2: Strategic Analysis

#### 2.1 SWOT Analysis

```
STRENGTHS                    │  WEAKNESSES
─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────
1. [Market position]         │ 1. [Product gap]
2. [Technology advantage]    │ 2. [Go-to-market weakness]
3. [Brand equity]            │ 3. [Customer satisfaction issue]
4. [Talent / team]           │ 4. [Resource constraint]
                             │
OPPORTUNITIES                │  THREATS
─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────
1. [Market trend to exploit] │ 1. [New entrants]
2. [Expansion path]          │ 2. [Regulatory risk]
3. [Acquisition targets]     │ 3. [Technology disruption]
4. [Partnership potential]   │ 4. [Our strategic moves]
```

#### 2.2 Strategic Group Map

Map competitors on two key dimensions:
- **X-axis:** [e.g., Product Breadth → Product Depth]
- **Y-axis:** [e.g., SMB Focus → Enterprise Focus]

Position each competitor in the resulting quadrants to identify:
- **Direct competitors** — same quadrant, immediate threat
- **Adjacent competitors** — different quadrant but overlapping use cases
- **Aspirational competitors** — where they're heading based on roadmap/strategy

#### 2.3 Porter's Five Forces

| Force | Intensity (1-5) | Key Factors |

### Phase 3: Actionable Intelligence

#### 3.1 Intelligence Briefing Template

```
COMPETITIVE INTELLIGENCE BRIEF
Date: [Date] | Analyst: [Name] | Confidentiality: [Level]

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
[3-5 bullet points of the most actionable findings]

KEY FINDINGS
1. [Finding] — Impact: [High/Med/Low] — Action: [Recommendation]
2. [Finding] — Impact: [High/Med/Low] — Action: [Recommendation]

RECOMMENDED ACTIONS
- Product: [Specific change / roadmap adjustment]
- Marketing: [Messaging / positioning update]
- Sales: [Battle card update / training needed]
- Strategy: [Market entry / exit / pricing change]

MONITORING TRIGGERS
- [Event to watch for and why]
- [Signal that would change our assessment]
```

#### 3.2 Intelligence Cadence

| Frequency | Deliverable | Audience |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| **Weekly** | Competitive newsletter / digest | Extended team |
| **Monthly** | Competitor deep-dive (1 competitor) | Product, Marketing |
| **Quarterly** | Landscape review + strategic implications | Executive team |
| **Trigger-based** | Flash alert on major competitor moves | Leadership |

## AI-Powered Competitive Analysis

Modern platforms automate large portions of this framework:

- **Continuous Monitoring** — [FollowEngine](https://followengine.com) tracks competitor websites, product changes, pricing updates, job postings, and review sentiment automatically
- **LLM Analysis** — AI-generated SWOT summaries, battle card drafts, and market landscape narratives
- **Alert Systems** — Real-time notifications on competitor launches, funding, and strategic moves

By combining structured analysis frameworks with automated intelligence gathering, CI teams shift from data collection to strategic insight generation.
